# Deal note: The Lord of the Rings collection

The Lord of the Rings collection is our strongest franchise target, and it ranks first in both views we ran, all years and 2000 onward. All three films were released from 2001, so Finance's doubts about older box-office reporting do not touch it. Returns here mean profit as a multiple of production budget: a return of 4 means the film earned five times its budget. Taken together, the three films returned about 10.0, the highest of the 23 franchise bundles we could assess; the typical bundle returned about 4.6, and the typical standalone film about 4.0. That gap is small next to how much bundles vary, so the number that drove our view is not the average but the weakest film: even the weakest Lord of the Rings film returned about 8.37, where the next-best bundle's weakest film (Ice Age) returned about 5.50. That answers the worry that a bundle means paying for weak middle entries. 

From 2000 onward the picture holds: the typical bundle returned 4.08 against 3.49 for the typical standalone film (12 films), and Lord of the Rings still leads on weakest-film return. We would pay a premium over the typical bundle, but we cannot turn a box-office return into a price, because it is not what a licence earns on StreamVault and we hold no prices. We would use the weakest-film return as the reason to prefer this bundle in the room, and anchor our walk-away point to that 8.37x floor: we should not pay above an 8.37x multiple, because at that price, a weaker middle entry drags the whole bundle below the safety of our typical standalone ROI. 

If we are wrong, the cost is $14M committed to one franchise instead of spread across standalone hits, or a lost bundle that a competitor then promotes. What we could not stand behind: our data holds three films for this collection, and whether the studio's bundle contains more is unchecked; seven ids on the list could not be verified (five missing from TMDb, one invalid, one bad text); and the bundle figures combine the money of all films in the bundle, while the standalone figures are per film, so the two are not strictly like for like.
